Mold Prevention Checklist for New York Homes

A room-by-room checklist for reducing indoor humidity and catching hidden moisture before it becomes a mold problem in New York's climate.

Why New York Homes Are Especially Susceptible

New York's humid summers and older housing stock create ideal mold conditions when combined with even minor, unresolved moisture. The EPA guidance on mold and moisture recommends keeping indoor relative humidity below 60%, and ideally between 30-50%, to meaningfully reduce mold risk regardless of climate.

Room-by-Room Checklist

Bathrooms: Run the exhaust fan during and 20 minutes after showers; check grout and caulk annually for gaps. Kitchens: Check under the sink monthly for slow leaks; ensure range hood ventilation exhausts outside, not into the attic. Basements: Use a dehumidifier during humid months; inspect for efflorescence or musty odor. Attics: Confirm roof and bathroom vents exhaust outside; check for daylight or staining near roof penetrations after storms.

When to Call a Professional

A DIY approach works for prevention, but visible mold growth larger than roughly 10 square feet, or any mold following a water loss that wasn't fully dried, should be handled by a certified remediation team rather than a household cleaner — see CDC mold and health guidance for health guidance on when professional remediation is warranted.
